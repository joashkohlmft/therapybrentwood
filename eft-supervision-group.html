Who this group is for

This cohort is designed for therapists who have completed at least Core Skills 1 & 2 and want a warm, supportive, collaborative space to deepen their Emotionally Focused Couples Therapy (EFCT). Together we'll explore what it means to bring a multicultural orientation to EFT with couples from diverse backgrounds.

Not quite there in your training yet, or unsure whether you're the right fit? Reach out anyway. I'd rather have a real conversation with you than have you count yourself out.

What we'll do together

The heart of this group is watching real work and learning from it, side by side. Each session offers space to bring your cases, share a recording, receive thoughtful feedback, try interventions out loud, and connect with fellow EFT therapists navigating the same beautiful, complex work.

  • Share and consult on live cases, with attention to the cultural context of each couple
  • Watch couple-session recordings and offer feedback grounded in curiosity, not critique
  • Practice interventions through role-play, seeing ideas come to life in the moment
  • Strengthen case conceptualization and tend to self-of-the-therapist blocks
  • Build a community of support that continues beyond any single session

On sharing your work

I warmly encourage you to share a recording of one of your couple sessions when you feel ready. Watching your actual work together, rather than only talking about it, is where the deepest learning happens. It lets the group offer you accurate, specific feedback on your case conceptualization and your self-of-the-therapist work: the reflecting, the pacing, the moments you reach and the moments you hold back.

This is always an invitation, never a requirement, and it moves entirely at your pace. When someone shares, we watch with care and respond the way we'd want our own work met: "Here's what stood out as effective…" and "Here's something I might have considered…" Then we often role-play those ideas together, so everyone gets to see the intervention come alive.

As we watch one another's work, we hold questions like:

  • What stood out to you as particularly effective?
  • Is there anything you might have approached differently, and what was your thinking?
  • How is culture, identity, or context alive in this moment, for the couple and for the therapist?

A few honest questions

Do I have to share a recording to take part?

No. Sharing a couple-session recording is warmly encouraged because it's where the richest feedback lives, but it's always your choice and always at your own pace. You'll gain a great deal simply by bringing cases and engaging with your colleagues' work.

What level of EFT training do I need?

This group is designed for therapists who have completed at least Core Skills 1 & 2. If you're earlier in your EFT journey but curious, reach out and we'll find the right fit for you, whether that's this cohort or a future one.

What if I can't make one of the dates?

That's okay. If a session or two doesn't work, you're welcome to join and catch up with the group. If the day and time don't fit at all, let me know you're interested and I'll keep you in mind for the next cohort.

What does "multicultural orientation" actually mean here?

It points to the Multicultural Orientation (MCO) model, the framework I lean on, developed by Davis, Owen, Hook, and DeBlaere. Rather than treating culture as a special topic, MCO holds it as always present, through three ways of being: cultural humility (an open, other-oriented stance that never assumes superiority about anyone's background), cultural opportunities (noticing and gently stepping into the moments where culture and identity surface in the room), and cultural comfort (growing our own ease in those conversations rather than avoiding them). We bring all three to how we watch one another's work and how we sit with couples, so that every background, including yours, is met with respect.
