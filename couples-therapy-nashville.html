Couples counseling and relationship therapy in Nashville, TN

Nashville is a city that demands a lot from the people who live here. Whether you work in healthcare at Vanderbilt or HCA, in the music industry, in one of the corporate offices that have helped make Nashville one of America's fastest-growing cities, or simply in the relentless rhythm of raising a family in a place that never stops moving, the pressure builds. And it often lands in your relationship first.

Most Nashville couples who reach out are not in open crisis so much as slowly worn down. They have built a good life together and can run the logistics of it without missing a beat, calendars, childcare, the mortgage, but the closeness that made the effort feel worth it has quietly thinned out. They operate like a well-run team and feel, privately, like strangers to each other.

Nashville also has a distinctive quality that affects couples more than most cities acknowledge: it is a city of transplants. Many couples here have no extended family nearby, no longstanding community roots, and no built-in support network. When the relationship is under strain, there is often no one to turn to. The distance between two people who are already each other's primary support becomes especially heavy.

Couples counseling and marriage therapy exist for exactly these moments, not to fix a failing relationship, but to rebuild the emotional bond that makes the whole thing feel worthwhile again.

The particular strains of a Nashville marriage

Two forces shape relationships here in ways they do not in most of the country, and both tend to land quietly on a marriage.

The first is Music City itself. Nashville runs on a creative economy built around the road: touring, session work, writers' rounds, late nights, and long stretches where one partner is gone and the other holds everything together at home. Income arrives in unpredictable bursts, a partner's work is often public, and the line between professional and personal blurs in a way few other careers demand. Even couples outside the industry feel its rhythm, the odd hours, the hustle, the sense that everyone around them is chasing something. Country music is full of marriages that did not survive the touring life and full of ones that did, and the difference is rarely luck. It is whether two people kept finding their way back to each other between the absences.

The second is faith. Nashville sits in the buckle of the Bible Belt, home to major denominations, seminaries, and publishing houses, and for a great many couples here marriage is not only a relationship but a covenant with real spiritual weight. That can be a deep source of commitment. It can also make struggle harder to admit: when your community, your church, and your own convictions all hold marriage as sacred, needing help can feel like failing at something you were supposed to get right. Some couples also carry the quieter legacy of purity-culture teaching, where years of messaging about sex outside marriage leave real obstacles to intimacy inside it. Nashville-based LifeWay Research has documented a genuine paradox here: Bible Belt divorce rates run higher than the region's strong marriage ideals would suggest, shaped by factors like younger marriage and, some argue, expectations so high that ordinary struggle starts to feel like failure. The point is not that faith fails couples, but that few are ever told a good marriage still takes repair.

I spent years as a pastoral counselor before training as an EFT therapist, so faith-shaped marriages are familiar ground for me. I do not require faith, push it, or judge it, whatever your relationship to religion. But if it matters to you, I understand the particular pressures and hopes it brings to a marriage, and I can hold that alongside the clinical work rather than around it.

Why Nashville couples choose online couples therapy

Nashville's traffic is not a small inconvenience. Anyone who has driven I-65 during a Predators game, or tried to get through Green Hills on a Friday afternoon, or navigated the I-24/I-40 interchange at the wrong time of day knows what it costs. For couples already stretched thin between work, parenting, and a city that never slows down, adding a 90-minute round trip to a couples therapy appointment is often what keeps people from starting at all.

Online couples counseling removes that barrier entirely.

Sessions take place via a secure, HIPAA-compliant video platform, private, professional, and accessible from anywhere in Tennessee. Both partners join from the same location or from separate devices. The research on this is clear: telehealth couples therapy produces outcomes equivalent to in-person work. The modality does not limit what is possible in the therapeutic relationship.

For Nashville couples, online therapy also offers something harder to quantify: discretion. There is no waiting room. No one sees your car in the parking lot. Therapy happens in the privacy of your own home, on a schedule that works around irregular hours, shift work, or a life that does not fit neatly into a 9-to-5 window.

Choosing a couples therapist in Nashville: why the approach matters

Nashville has no shortage of therapists, and "couples counseling," "marriage counseling," and "relationship therapy" can point to very different work depending on who is in the room and how they trained. The strongest predictor of whether therapy actually helps is not the label on the door but the model the therapist practices, and how deeply they were trained to deliver it.

I work exclusively from Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT), the couples approach with the deepest body of outcome research in the field. Rather than repeat the whole model here, I have laid it out in plain language elsewhere: what EFT actually is, how it differs from the Gottman Method, and the full overview of EFT couples therapy. What matters when you are choosing among Nashville therapists is this: EFT does not stop at communication tips or a toolkit of conflict rules. It works on the emotional bond itself, whether you each feel your partner is truly there for you when it counts, which is what changes how two people treat each other in the ordinary moments of a week.

75% of couples reach full recovery from relationship distress through EFT, among the highest rates recorded for any couples approach (ICEEFT Research Summary), and those gains hold, continuing to grow at two-year follow-up.

Triple certification, unique in the Nashville area

I am ICEEFT Triple-Certified in EFT for couples (EFCT), individuals (EFIT), and families (EFFT), a designation held by fewer than several hundred therapists worldwide. I am also an EFT Supervisor Candidate, supervising other therapists in the EFT model. I am not aware of another couples counselor or marriage therapist currently serving the Nashville/Brentwood area who holds triple certification from ICEEFT.

Certification is not a marketing term. ICEEFT certification requires hundreds of supervised clinical hours, video review of actual therapy sessions by certified trainers, and demonstrated competence at each stage of the EFT model. It is the difference between a therapist who has read about EFT and one who has been trained and evaluated in it.

Starting couples therapy from anywhere in Nashville

We start with a free 20-minute video consultation, low-stakes and unhurried, a first conversation about what is going on and whether I am the right fit for the two of you. If we decide to work together, sessions run 50 minutes, weekly or every other week, joined from wherever you are: the living room once the kids are down, two separate screens when one of you is traveling for work, even a parked car on a lunch break if that is what the week allows.

Early on, my job is to help you both see the loop you keep falling into, the particular sequence of reactions that leaves each of you feeling worse, and to turn that loop, rather than each other, into the thing we push against together. Most couples feel some relief the moment the pattern is named out loud. From there we work toward the softer, more vulnerable feelings sitting underneath it, and the way you respond to each other starts to shift.

Most couples notice meaningful change within 8 to 12 sessions. When things are acute, a recent affair, a rupture that cannot wait, or years of distance you would rather not stretch out over months, a couples therapy intensive can concentrate months of weekly work into a few focused days. If Brentwood is the easier drive, I run the same work as a couples therapy intensive in Brentwood, in person or online.

I keep fees flat and transparent, the same rate whether you come as a couple or an individual, with 60- and 90-minute options. Common questions

Do you offer couples counseling for Nashville couples?

Yes. I offer online couples counseling for Nashville couples throughout Davidson County and across Tennessee via secure telehealth. No office visit is required. Sessions are conducted via a private, HIPAA-compliant video platform and are accessible from anywhere in the state.

How does online couples therapy work for Nashville couples?

Both partners join a secure video session from the same location or from separate devices. Sessions are 50 minutes, held weekly or bi-weekly. Research consistently shows that telehealth couples therapy produces outcomes equivalent to in-person work, the modality does not limit what is possible in the therapeutic relationship.

Is online therapy as effective as in-person therapy?

Yes. Multiple peer-reviewed studies have found that telehealth therapy produces outcomes equivalent to in-person therapy across a wide range of presenting concerns, including couples work. For many Nashville couples, online therapy is actually more accessible, no commute, flexible scheduling around irregular work hours, and sessions conducted in the privacy of your own home.

Can my partner and I join from two different locations?

Yes, and many Nashville couples do. If one of you travels for work, commutes to another city, or simply cannot be in the same room that evening, you can each join the session from your own device. It is one of the practical advantages of online couples therapy: the work continues even when your schedules or locations do not line up.

Which Nashville-area communities do you serve?

Because sessions are online, I work with couples anywhere in Tennessee, including across greater Nashville: Davidson County, Brentwood, Franklin, and Williamson County, plus Mt. Juliet, Hendersonville, Nolensville, Spring Hill, and Murfreesboro. Wherever you are in the state, you attend from home. For couples specifically weighing EFT against other models, I compare the two leading approaches on the EFT vs. Gottman page.

How many couples counseling sessions will we need?

Most Nashville couples begin to feel meaningful shifts within 8 to 12 sessions, though the pace depends on what you are carrying in. Couples facing an acute crisis, infidelity, a sudden rupture, or years of built-up distance, sometimes choose a couples therapy intensive instead, concentrating months of progress into a few focused days.

I keep fees flat and transparent, the same rate for individuals and couples, with 60- and 90-minute options and multi-day intensives. Current session and intensive rates are listed on the fees section of my FAQ. I work out of network and can provide a monthly superbill for possible out-of-network reimbursement, and sessions are HSA and FSA eligible.
