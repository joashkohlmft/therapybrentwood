What is couples therapy?

Couples therapy, also called marriage counseling or relationship therapy, is a form of psychotherapy that helps partners understand and change the patterns keeping them stuck in conflict, distance, or disconnection. I offer Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT) for couples throughout Tennessee via telehealth, the most extensively researched couples therapy approach in the world, with a 75% full recovery rate in distressed couples, among the highest rates recorded for any couples therapy modality, according to the ICEEFT research summary.

What brings most couples to therapy?

Whether you call it marriage counseling, couples counseling, or relationship therapy, what brings most couples here is the same. They feel disconnected. Stuck, repeating the same arguments, feeling unseen, walking on eggshells, or watching the distance between them grow wider every year.

For high-achieving couples, there is often an additional layer: you have built an impressive life together, careers, children, financial security, and yet somewhere along the way you became excellent partners in logistics and strangers in intimacy. The same analytical drive that makes you exceptional professionally can make emotional connection feel elusive. You can diagnose the problem clearly and still not know how to feel your way through it.

You might recognize some of these patterns:

  • The same fight keeps happening no matter what the topic is
  • One of you pursues and the other withdraws, and neither feels good
  • Emotional or physical intimacy has faded and you don't know how to get it back
  • A betrayal or attachment injury has left one or both of you feeling unsafe
  • You love each other but feel more like roommates than partners
  • You're parenting together but have lost the connection that brought you together

These patterns are not character flaws. They are attachment responses, signals that something important is not being felt or heard. And they can change.

How does Emotionally Focused Therapy work?

Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT) is widely considered the gold standard in couples therapy, the most extensively researched approach in the world, backed by 40 years of clinical trials and one of the few couples approaches with neurological evidence of lasting change. Unlike approaches that focus primarily on communication skills or behavioral change, EFT works at the level of attachment, the deep emotional bond that shapes how we feel safe, seen, and loved.

In EFT, we explore, with curiosity, beneath the surface of conflict, communication breakdowns, emotional disconnection, and recurring arguments. The goal is not just conflict resolution but something deeper: to rebuild trust and reconnect in a way that lasts. We identify the negative cycle you're caught in, the moves and countermoves that leave both of you feeling worse, and we work to understand what's driving it. Usually it's fear. Fear of rejection, fear of being too much, fear of not mattering.

When those deeper emotions are accessed and expressed safely, something shifts. Partners begin to respond to each other differently, with curiosity instead of defensiveness, with tenderness instead of withdrawal. The cycle changes. The bond deepens.

86–90% of couples who complete EFT see significant, measurable improvement, even among those who don't reach full recovery. And crucially, those gains hold: research consistently shows EFT outcomes are sustained and continue to deepen at two-year follow-up. Couples do not revert to old patterns after therapy ends.
What does "gold standard" actually mean? When the APA designates a treatment as the gold standard in psychotherapy research, it means the evidence has been replicated so consistently, across independent research teams, different countries, and different populations, that it becomes the benchmark everything else is measured against. ICEEFT states that EFT has earned the APA's highest research designation, having "systematically covered all the factors set out in optimal models of psychotherapy research" over 30 years. EFT's evidence base is also growing globally: results have been replicated with Chinese-speaking couples in Canada, Spanish-speaking couples across Argentina, Costa Rica, Guatemala, Mexico, and Spain, and studies are actively underway in Uganda, Turkey, Hungary, and Brazil. The attachment science at its foundation is universal, and the global evidence is increasingly confirming it.

Infidelity & betrayal recovery

Few experiences shake a relationship to its foundation like infidelity or betrayal. Affair recovery and relationship repair after trust has broken creates an attachment injury that can feel impossible to move past. Trust is shattered. Safety is gone. And yet, with the right support, many couples do recover, and emerge with a relationship that is stronger and more honest than it was before.

I am trained in the Attachment Injury Resolution Model and bring specialized experience in helping couples navigate the painful but transformative process of betrayal recovery. This work is not about minimizing what happened. It is about helping both partners understand the underlying vulnerabilities in the relationship, process the wound with honesty and compassion, and decide together what they want to build.

This work takes courage from both partners. It is not quick. But it is possible.

Pornography, addiction & sexual betrayal

Pornography addiction and compulsive sexual behavior are among the most painful and least-discussed issues couples bring to therapy. The partner who discovers habitual pornography use often experiences it as a profound betrayal. The partner using pornography often carries deep shame and has rarely had a safe space to examine what drives the behavior. I work with both partners, helping each understand what has happened, process hurt and shame, and rebuild a relationship grounded in genuine intimacy rather than secrecy.

Grief in relationships

Loss, the death of a child or parent, miscarriage, infertility, the loss of a career or a dream, can pull partners apart rather than together, especially when each person grieves differently. I help couples navigate shared and individual grief in a way that deepens rather than fractures their connection.

What can I expect from couples therapy?

We begin with a complimentary 20-minute consultation to see if we're a good fit. From there, couples typically meet weekly or bi-weekly for 50-minute sessions.

In the early sessions, I focus on understanding your specific cycle, the recurring pattern of moves and countermoves that leaves you both feeling worse. I name it, help you both see it, and externalize it so the cycle becomes the shared problem rather than each other. Most couples find this reframe brings immediate relief.

As therapy progresses, sessions go deeper, accessing the fears and longings underneath the reactive behaviors. This is where real change happens. You begin to hear each other differently. Defenses soften. Partners who have felt invisible begin to be seen. Partners who have shut down begin to open up.

Most couples begin to feel meaningful shifts within 8–12 sessions. Sessions are conducted online via a secure, HIPAA-compliant platform, from anywhere in Tennessee, from the privacy of your own space.

Common questions

How effective is EFT couples therapy?

EFT is widely considered the gold standard in couples therapy research. Clinical trials show that 75 percent of couples achieve full recovery from relationship distress, among the highest rates recorded for any couples therapy modality (ICEEFT Research Summary). Gains are sustained and continue growing at two-year follow-up.

How is EFT couples therapy different from other approaches?

EFT works not by teaching communication skills but by going directly to the emotional reality driving conflict. It is one of the few couples therapy approaches with neurological evidence, a landmark brain scan study showed that after EFT, partners literally register each other as a biological safe haven at a brain level.

Do you offer couples therapy in Brentwood and Nashville TN?

Yes. I offer EFT couples therapy throughout Tennessee via secure telehealth, including Brentwood, Nashville, and surrounding areas. I am triple-certified by ICEEFT in EFT for couples, individuals, and families.

Can EFT couples therapy help with infidelity and affair recovery?

Yes. Infidelity and betrayal create what EFT calls an attachment injury, a rupture so significant it can feel impossible to move past. With the right support, many couples do recover from affairs and emerge with a relationship that is stronger and more honest than what existed before. I offer specialized affair recovery work within the EFT framework throughout Tennessee via telehealth.

How many couples therapy sessions will we need?

Most couples begin to feel meaningful shifts within 8 to 12 sessions, though the depth and pace vary by couple. Sessions are typically 50 minutes, held weekly or bi-weekly. Couples who are highly distressed or have complex histories may benefit from a therapy intensive, a concentrated multi-day format that compresses months of progress into a single focused experience.
