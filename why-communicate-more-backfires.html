Why "Just Communicate More" Backfires for Neurodivergent Couples

Written by Joash Koh, LMFT · ICEEFT Certified EFT Therapist · Brentwood, Tennessee

In brief

  • ·Standard advice like "just communicate more" assumes both partners process the world the same way. For neurodivergent couples, it is often aimed at the wrong target.
  • ·The problem is rarely a lack of love. It is signals that get sent in one language and read in another.
  • ·Sensory overload is often the hidden trigger. A fight usually starts in the body, before anyone says a word.
  • ·A narrower window of tolerance means overload arrives sooner. Going slow, and reading shutdown as overwhelm, is what lets a neurodivergent couple actually reconnect.

The advice that keeps missing

If you are in a relationship where one or both of you is neurodivergent, you have probably been handed the standard advice more than once. Communicate more. Make eye contact. Just try harder to remember. And you have probably noticed that it does not help, and sometimes it leaves the neurodivergent partner feeling like the broken one all over again.

There is a reason for that. Most relationship advice assumes both partners take in emotion, sound, and conversation the same way. When your brains are wired differently, that advice is aimed at the wrong target. What follows is how I understand what is actually happening underneath your fights, and why a different approach works better.

Your wiring is a difference, not a defect

I start from a simple place: ADHD and Autism are variations in how a human nervous system works, not character flaws. The neurodivergent partner is the expert on their own inner world, on how their brain handles closeness, attention, and overwhelm. My job is not to talk you out of that experience. It is to help your partner understand it from the inside, and to help you both build around it.

That stance matters, because so much of the pain in these relationships comes from years of being told, directly or not, that you are too much, too distant, too forgetful, too sensitive. We are not adding to that list.

The cycle is the problem, not your partner

Here is the shift that changes everything. The "forgetful" partner and the "aloof" partner are not the problem. The two of you are caught in a cycle, a loop of moves and counter-moves that runs on its own once it gets going. If you have never seen it named, the pursuer-withdrawer cycle is the most common version.

Executive function differences and sensory overload act like a trigger. When a neurodivergent nervous system hits capacity, the alarm goes off, and that alarm reaches straight into the attachment system, the part of us that asks "are you still there, do I still matter?" A very real biological moment turns into a very real relationship moment. Once you can see the loop as the thing you are both up against, you stop being each other's enemy.

When the system floods

One partner goes quiet

Blank face, short answers, or leaving the room. From the outside it can look like stonewalling or not caring.

From the inside, it is a nervous system that has hit capacity and is trying to stay safe.

When closeness feels at risk

One partner reaches harder

Repeating, explaining, raising the stakes. From the outside it can look like "too much."

From the inside, it is often rejection sensitivity and a bid to know you still matter.

The signals get scrambled

A lot of the hurt between neurodivergent partners is not about the absence of love. It is about signals that get sent in one language and read in another.

One partner shuts down because their system is flooded, and the other reads it as "you do not care about me." One partner forgets something important, and it lands as "I am not a priority." Neither reading is true, but both are understandable. Much of the work is translation: helping the flooded partner learn to send a clear, honest signal ("I am overwhelmed, I am not leaving") and helping the other partner learn to hear the overwhelmed nervous system behind the silence.

This is slow, careful work on purpose. I keep things soft, simple, and specific, and I use your words rather than mine, because a neurodivergent system often has a narrow window before it floods. Going slow is not padding. It is what keeps both of you present enough to actually reach each other.

The problem is rarely that you don't love each other.

It's that the signal was sent in one language and read in another.

Sensory overload is often the hidden trigger

People tend to think a fight starts with the words. Often it starts in the body, before anyone says anything.

A sharp change in tone. Too much noise, or clutter, or a scent in the room. Sustained eye contact when the system is already full. A well-meant reach for a hand at exactly the wrong moment. For a neurodivergent nervous system, any of these can be the small spike of stress that tips things over, and by the time the words start, the alarm is already ringing.

Part of what we do together is learn to notice this earlier. Not through abstract emotion labels, which can be hard to reach in the moment, but through the tangible signs: the tight chest, the headache, the foggy blankness, the jittery, fast-talking energy. Those are your nervous system telling you it is trying to stay safe. When you can both read them, you can slow down before the cycle takes over, instead of after.

The window of tolerance, and why it's narrower

There is a simple idea from trauma and nervous-system work that makes all of this concrete: the window of tolerance. It is the range in which your nervous system can stay calm, present, and able to connect. Inside the window, you can hear your partner, think, and repair. Above it, you tip into hyperarousal, the protest, pursuit, and panic. Below it, you drop into hypoarousal, the shutdown, freeze, and going blank.

Many neurodivergent nervous systems have a narrower window. The same conversation that leaves one partner still inside their window can push the other straight out of it. That is not fragility or drama. It is wiring. And it is why "just push through and keep talking" so often backfires: you are asking a system that has already left the window to perform as if it were still inside.

Reading shutdown as overwhelm rather than rejection is the single most useful shift a neurodivergent couple can make. It turns a wall into a signal you can work with.

What a session built for your nervous system feels like

Because sensory load matters, the room itself matters. I pay attention to pace and tone, keep questions from coming at you out of nowhere, and never require eye contact to prove you are engaged. When something abstract needs to become clear, I will often make it concrete, on paper or as a simple map, rather than expecting you to hold it all in your head.

The goal is to keep the sensory input low enough that both of you can stay reachable. You cannot do the tender, honest work of repair while your system is bracing for the next spike. This is the same reason the usual cycle is so hard to interrupt with willpower alone: willpower lives inside the window, and by the time you are fighting, one of you has usually left it.

You are each other's regulation

Here is the part that gives me the most hope for these relationships. A safe connection with your partner is not a nice extra. It is one of the most powerful ways a human nervous system calms down.

When the two of you can reach each other, when a reach is met with "I am here," the threatened brain settles. And a settled brain manages ADHD and Autism far better than a flooded one. This is the quiet engine underneath the whole approach, an idea that runs through the research behind this model: we are built to steady each other. For a neurodivergent couple, that is not a slogan. It is a resource you can learn to use.

You were never too much, or too distant. You were two nervous systems trying to reach each other across a gap no one had named.

Named and understood, that gap gets smaller. I have watched a partner's silence stop reading as rejection, and felt the whole cycle go quiet.

That is the work I do, and it is built for how the two of you are actually wired.
