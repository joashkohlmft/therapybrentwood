Trauma Therapy & Brainspotting · Brentwood, TN · Online Sessions

Trauma lives in the body — healing begins there too

As a Certified Clinical Trauma Professional (CCTP) and Brainspotting practitioner, I offer specialized, body-informed trauma therapy that goes deeper than talk — reaching the places where trauma is actually stored.

What is trauma therapy and Brainspotting?

Trauma therapy is a specialized form of counseling that helps people heal from the lasting effects of overwhelming experiences — including PTSD, childhood trauma, complex trauma, and emotional wounds that continue to shape how you feel and relate. Brainspotting is a neuroscience-based method developed by Dr. David Grand that accesses and processes trauma stored beneath conscious awareness, in the subcortical brain and body. Joash Koh LMFT, CCTP offers body-based trauma therapy including Brainspotting throughout Tennessee via telehealth.

What trauma actually is

Trauma is not just what happened to you. It is what happened inside you in response — the way your nervous system adapted to survive an experience that felt overwhelming, threatening, or deeply unsafe. Those adaptations were brilliant at the time. But they often continue long after the danger has passed, shaping how you feel, how you relate, and how you move through the world.

Trauma can come from a single overwhelming event — an accident, an assault, a sudden loss. Or it can come from a lifetime of smaller ruptures — chronic emotional neglect, an unsafe childhood, years of living in a relationship that felt unpredictable or frightening. Both are real. Both deserve care.

Why talk therapy alone isn't always enough

Traditional talk therapy asks you to understand your experiences through language and logic. But trauma is stored subcortically — beneath the thinking brain, in the body's nervous system, muscles, and implicit memory. You can understand your trauma perfectly and still feel hijacked by it.

This is why I integrate body-based, somatic approaches alongside talk. We work with what your body is holding, not just what your mind can articulate.

Trauma therapy is not about re-living the past. It is about helping your nervous system learn — at a felt, embodied level — that the danger is over, and that you are safe now.

Brainspotting (BSP)

Brainspotting is a powerful, brain-body trauma processing approach developed by Dr. David Grand. It is based on the discovery that where you look affects how you feel — and that specific eye positions, or "brainspots," correlate with traumatic material held in the subcortical brain.

By finding and holding a relevant brainspot while accessing a traumatic memory or sensation, the brain is able to process and release what has been stuck — often going deeper and moving faster than traditional talk therapy alone. Brainspotting is particularly effective for:

  • Acute trauma and PTSD
  • Complex and developmental trauma
  • Attachment injuries
  • Somatic symptoms with no clear physical cause
  • Performance anxiety and creative blocks
  • Spiritual trauma and religious wounding
  • Sexual trauma and childhood sexual abuse
  • Grief that feels frozen, complicated, or stuck
  • Shame-based patterns rooted in trauma

Trauma-informed care in every session

Whether we use Brainspotting or not, every session I offer is trauma-informed. This means I work at your pace — never pushing faster than your nervous system can tolerate. It means I understand how trauma affects the body, the relational system, and the self. And it means I bring genuine attunement and care to the parts of you that may never have felt truly safe enough to be seen.

Sexual trauma

Sexual trauma requires a particularly careful, body-informed, and deeply relational approach. Many survivors have spent years minimizing what happened or carrying it entirely alone. I create a slow, safe, attuned therapeutic space where survivors can begin to process at their own pace — in their body, their emotions, and their sense of self — without ever being pushed faster than they are ready to go.

Common questions

What is Brainspotting (BSP)?

Brainspotting is a trauma-processing modality developed by Dr. David Grand that uses the visual field to locate, focus, and process trauma stored in the subcortical brain. It works below the level of language — reaching the body-held experiences that talk therapy alone cannot fully access. It is particularly effective for trauma, PTSD, anxiety, and somatic symptoms.

How is Brainspotting different from EMDR?

Both Brainspotting and EMDR use eye positioning to access trauma. Brainspotting tends to be more open-ended and body-focused, allowing the brain to process at its own pace, while EMDR uses more structured bilateral stimulation protocols. Both are evidence-informed approaches to trauma processing.

What is trauma and how does it affect the body?

Trauma is not just what happened to you — it is what happened inside you in response. When an experience feels overwhelming or deeply unsafe, the nervous system adapts to survive. Those adaptations were brilliant at the time, but they often continue long after the danger has passed, shaping how you feel, how you relate, and how you move through the world. This is why trauma therapy must address the body, not just the mind — the nervous system holds the pattern, and healing requires reaching it there.

Why isn't talk therapy always enough for trauma?

Talk therapy works through language and conscious reflection — both of which are processed in the cortex. Trauma, however, is stored in subcortical structures that predate language: the amygdala, the brainstem, the body itself. This is why you can understand your trauma intellectually and still be triggered, still freeze, still feel the fear as though it is happening now. Body-based approaches like Brainspotting access the subcortical processing that talk alone cannot reach.
