Neurodivergent Couples Therapy · ADHD & Autism · Tennessee

When neurodivergence meets love

Couples therapy for partners navigating ADHD and Autism, especially when the diagnosis came later in life, and you are trying to feel close again. You do not need to be fixed. You need someone who understands both your neurology and your bond, and how the two shape each other.

When one or both of you is wired differently

If one or both of you has recently learned, or started to suspect, that your brain is wired differently, you may be looking back at your whole relationship and seeing it in a new light. The forgotten anniversary. The way one of you goes silent in the middle of a hard conversation. The feeling of asking and asking and never quite being heard. For a long time those moments probably got read as not caring, or not trying hard enough. They were never about love or effort. They were about wiring.

Learning that can bring relief and grief in the same breath, and both of those belong here. This is couples therapy built for how the two of you are actually made, not for an average that fits neither of you.

Why this work needs someone who does both

Most couples therapy quietly assumes that both partners take in emotion, sound, and conversation the same way. When you do not, the standard advice, "just communicate more," "make more eye contact," does not just fall flat. It can make things worse, and it can leave the neurodivergent partner feeling, once again, like the problem.

A late ADHD or Autism diagnosis reframes years of history. That reframe deserves a therapist who can hold the neurology and the attachment bond at the same time, rather than treating your relationship like a communication problem to drill.

You do not need to be fixed. You need a bond that fits your two brains, not one that makes either of you perform "normal."

Why Emotionally Focused Therapy fits

Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT) is one of the most researched approaches to couples work, and it is built entirely around attachment: the deep human need to know that your partner is there, that they will respond, and that you matter to them.

Being neurodivergent does not change that you need those things. It changes how they get sent, how they get missed, and how they get repaired. That is exactly the level EFT works on. Instead of managing symptoms one by one, we find the negative cycle underneath the conflict, so the two of you can stop fighting each other and start facing the pattern together. You can read more about the model itself in what EFT is, and about the loop most couples get caught in through the pursuer-withdrawer cycle.

How I read your cycle differently

When one of you goes quiet, it is easy to hear it as being shut out. For an autistic or ADHD partner, going quiet is often a nervous system that has hit capacity, not a door closing on you. We slow things down until that shutdown gets read as overwhelm, not indifference.

And the partner who keeps reaching is not "too much." A lifetime of rejection sensitivity, of feeling half a step behind, of bracing for the next sign of being wrong, can turn a simple bid for closeness into a wave of protest. We make sense of the protest instead of treating it as the problem.

Together we map how each of your brains shapes your protective moves, so you finally understand what your partner is actually doing when the cycle takes over. Then you can respond to the person in front of you, not the pattern.

What working together looks like

A pace and a setting that respect how you process. No ambush questions, no forced eye contact, room to think before you answer.

Language that names neurodivergent experience accurately and without shame, for both of you. A focus on building a bond that fits your two brains, not on getting the neurodivergent partner to perform "normal." And practical, attachment-based ways to signal "I am here, I have got you" that actually land for how each of you is wired.

If you want the fuller picture of why the usual advice backfires and what I do instead, I wrote about it here: why "just communicate more" backfires for neurodivergent couples.

A note on where I am coming from

I am a late-diagnosed ADHD adult myself. I know the particular mix of relief and grief that follows a late diagnosis, and how it can ripple through a relationship. I have also worked with neurodivergent people one to one, so I understand executive function, rejection sensitivity, sensory overload, and shutdown as inside experiences, not just relationship behaviors. That is part of why I can help one partner see the overloaded nervous system behind the other's silence, instead of reading it as rejection.

Plenty of therapists understand attachment. Some understand neurodivergence. Working fluently in both at once is rarer, and it is the combination this work asks for.

Common questions

What is neurodivergent couples therapy?

It is couples work designed for partners where one or both are ADHD, autistic, or otherwise wired differently. Instead of treating a difference as a deficit, it accounts for how each of you processes emotion, sensory input, and conversation. I use Emotionally Focused Therapy, an attachment-based approach, and adapt the pace and sensory environment so both nervous systems can stay present enough to reconnect.

Does EFT work for ADHD and autistic couples?

Yes. EFT is built around attachment, the need to feel your partner is there, will respond, and that you matter. Being neurodivergent does not change that you need those things; it changes how they get expressed, missed, and repaired. EFT works at exactly that level, finding the cycle underneath the conflict rather than drilling communication skills that fall apart when a nervous system is overwhelmed.

Why does generic couples advice make things worse for us?

Most advice assumes both partners take in emotion, sound, and conversation the same way. "Just communicate more" or "make eye contact" can raise the sensory load and leave the neurodivergent partner feeling like the problem. When one partner goes quiet from overload rather than indifference, pushing for more talk usually escalates the cycle instead of repairing it.

We just got a late diagnosis. Is now a good time?

A late diagnosis often reframes years of history, which can bring relief and grief at once. That is a meaningful time to do this work. Therapy can help both of you make sense of old hurts as wiring rather than a lack of love or effort, and rebuild a bond that fits how each of you is actually built.

Do you offer this online in Tennessee?

Yes. I work with couples throughout Tennessee via secure telehealth, including Brentwood, Nashville, Franklin, Knoxville, Memphis, and Chattanooga. Telehealth can also be easier for neurodivergent nervous systems, since you are in your own space with more control over the sensory environment.
