The Wondrous Art of Love: a free EFT guide for couples

Twenty-eight handouts drawn from Emotionally Focused Therapy and attachment science, written by a fellow marriage and family therapist whose work I trust. I share them with the couples I work with, and now with you. Free to download, no sign-up required.

What is this guide?

The Wondrous Art of Love is a collection of twenty-eight handouts for couples, written by a licensed marriage and family therapist and author whose work draws deeply on Emotionally Focused Therapy and attachment science, credited fully below. The handouts follow the journey couples actually travel: understanding the bond and the cycle beneath the fights, finding the softer emotions under the loud ones, learning to reach and to catch, healing the big hurts, and tending the connection, through storms, joys, and grief, for a lifetime.

Each page stands alone: a short teaching, a practice for the two of you, a note for when it’s hard, questions to sit with, and a shared intention for the week. You can use them in order as a curriculum, or reach for the one this season of your relationship needs.

I share this guide because it says, beautifully and accurately, what I spend my days helping couples discover in session. The clinical ideas inside it, the negative cycle, pursuer and withdrawer roles, raw spots, attachment injuries, reaching and responding, are the same ones I work with as an ICEEFT Certified EFT Therapist.

What’s inside: the 28 handouts

Here is the full journey, in the order the handouts travel it.

The bond and the cycle

  • Wired for Each Other. The science of the bond: why needing your partner is not weakness but biology.
  • The Chasm Isn’t What You Think. Beneath nearly every fight lives one question: are you there for me?
  • The Dance, Not the Dancers. Naming the negative cycle, and forming the true alliance: the two of you against the pattern.
  • The Pursuer and the Protector. The two roles in the dance, and the loyalty hidden inside each.
  • Beneath the Anger, Under the Silence. Surface emotions push partners away; the softer ones underneath pull them close.
  • Raw Spots. Why some reactions arrive with the voltage of every older wound they resemble.

When the alarm goes off

  • When Old Dragons Visit. Trauma’s echoes in the bond, and how a steady, non-judging presence helps the nervous system learn the war is over.
  • When the Alarm Goes Off. Attachment panic in the body: pausing without abandoning, and returning so the real conversation becomes possible.
  • Stopping the Music. De-escalation as teamwork: catching the cycle in the act, “I think we’re in it.”
  • The Question Under Everything. Accessible, responsive, engaged: the three ways to transmit a yes that actually lands.

Reaching and catching

  • The Reach. The difference between a protest and a reach: handing your partner your heart instead of an accusation.
  • Catching the Reach. Receiving before fixing, and retiring the Fixer, the Lawyer, and the Matcher.
  • The Softening. For the one who pursues: letting the fear beneath the protest finally speak. Fear invites, where anger indicts.
  • Coming Out of the Fortress. For the one who withdraws: sending signals through the wall, “overwhelmed, not gone.”

Healing and tending

  • Healing the Big Hurts. Attachment injuries and their repair: the wound needs a witness before it needs anything else.
  • Safe Haven, Secure Base. The two gifts of a secure bond: a shore to crash on and a shore to sail from.
  • The Language of Touch. Non-demand touch, the long embrace, and why emotional safety is the ground where physical intimacy regrows.
  • Rituals of Us. Small protected habits, the parting kiss, the evening check-in, that answer the deep question daily.

The story of us, for life

  • The Story of Us, Retold. Becoming the couple who knows the way back, and telling the story with the rescue in it.
  • When the Old Dance Starts Again. Relapse without despair: the measure of progress is how soon a hand reaches across.
  • Tending the Bond for Life. Seasonal tending, and the lifelong practice of reaching sooner than last time.

Going deeper

  • The Anatomy of a Feeling. George Faller’s TEMPO: Trigger, Emotion, Meaning, Protection, Organization, the five parts of every emotional moment.
  • How You Learned to Love. Your attachment blueprint, and how new experience in this bond can rewrite it.
  • Am I Worth Loving? How shame curates a lovable exhibit while the real self waits unvisited, and how being received is the cure.
  • The Other Dances. Beyond pursue-withdraw: fire against fire, and the polite drift where a bond starves quietly.

Through storms, joys, and grief

  • When the World Storms In. External pressure as the third party: naming the real enemy, and becoming us versus this.
  • Sharing the Joys. Turning toward the good news, and why joy, unlike grievance, doesn’t knock twice.
  • Grieving Together. Permission to mourn differently, and the vow: you will not carry it alone.

How to use these handouts

Use them however they serve you: in order as a twenty-eight-week curriculum, one skill per week, or one season at a time. Read them together at the kitchen table, or separately and compare notes. Each handout ends with a practice and a shared intention, so the reading turns into something you actually do together, not just something you agree with.

If you are currently in couples therapy, the handouts map closely onto the arc of EFT itself: de-escalating the cycle, restructuring how you reach for and respond to each other, and consolidating a bond that holds. Bring one to session if it names something you have not been able to say.

And remember what the guide itself says the pages are all reaching for: not a perfect relationship, but a reachable one. Not the end of hard nights, but two people who know the way back to each other.

About the author

Dr. Mark Gregory Karris is a licensed marriage and family therapist and author based in San Diego, California. His clinical work and writing draw deeply on Emotionally Focused Therapy, attachment science, and trauma-informed care, and his books on relationships and emotional healing have reached readers well beyond the therapy room.

I did not write this guide, and I want the credit to land where it belongs. What I can tell you, as an ICEEFT-certified EFT therapist who works with these exact concepts every day, is that the clinical substance here is sound.

Can a guide replace couples therapy?

No, and it doesn’t claim to. Handouts can do something real: they name the cycle, soften the blame, and give the two of you shared language for what has been happening between you. For some couples in a mild rough season, that alone changes the tone of things.

But restructuring an emotional bond, especially after betrayal, years of distance, or repeated failed repair, usually needs a trained third person in the room. If you read these pages and recognize your marriage in them, that recognition is worth taking seriously. I offer EFT couples therapy and multi-day intensives throughout Tennessee via secure telehealth, and a free 20-minute consultation if you want to talk about where to start.

Questions about the guide

Is the guide really free?

Yes. The full 28-handout PDF is free to download with no sign-up, no email address, and no strings attached.

Do we need to be in therapy to use it?

Not at all. The handouts were written to stand on their own. That said, they pair naturally with EFT couples therapy, and many couples find that the pages give them language they then bring into session.

Where should we start?

If you are new to these ideas, start at the beginning: Wired for Each Other and The Chasm Isn’t What You Think reframe what your fights are actually about. If you are in a hard season right now, go straight to Stopping the Music and When the Alarm Goes Off.

What is EFT, in a sentence?

Emotionally Focused Therapy is the most extensively researched couples therapy approach in the world; it helps partners understand the negative cycle keeping them stuck and rebuild their emotional bond from the inside out. I explain it fully in What Is EFT?
