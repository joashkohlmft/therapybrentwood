Couples therapy intensives in Brentwood, Tennessee

Brentwood runs on a certain kind of momentum. The careers are demanding, the calendars are full, and between work, school pickups around Cool Springs or Maryland Farms, and everything else, there is little room for a standing weekly appointment, let alone the slow, stop-and-start pace of traditional couples therapy. For a lot of Brentwood couples, that is exactly how the distance was allowed to grow: not because they stopped caring, but because there was never a real block of time to turn toward each other.

An intensive is built for that reality. Instead of squeezing your marriage into a 50-minute slot between meetings, you set aside two, three, or four days and do the real work in one focused container, either in person in a private Brentwood setting or online from anywhere in Tennessee. No commute back to the office mid-breakthrough. No waiting a week for the next session while the tension quietly resets.

I am Joash Koh, an ICEEFT triple-certified EFT therapist, and couples intensives are the center of my work. Being in the same room can matter for this kind of work, and for Brentwood's high-achieving couples, used to solving problems analytically, an intensive is often the first time the emotional work finally moves as fast as the rest of their lives.

What is a therapy intensive?

A therapy intensive is a concentrated format of therapy, typically a two-day, three-day, or four-day experience, that compresses months of weekly appointments into focused, uninterrupted time, allowing for deeper and faster progress than the traditional weekly model. I offer EFT-based couples intensives and individual intensives throughout Tennessee, over secure video or in person, in two-day, three-day, and four-day formats.

Why an intensive?

Weekly therapy is powerful. But it has a limitation, you spend 50 minutes going deep, and then you go back to your life, your work, your routines. The momentum builds slowly.

Intensives work differently. By carving out dedicated, uninterrupted time for the therapeutic work, we can go further, faster, without ordinary life pulling you back before something important has shifted. For high-achieving couples and individuals with demanding schedules, this format often fits better than committing to months of weekly appointments.

For couples especially, I recommend a minimum of two consecutive days, and often three or four. The first day opens things up; the deeper days are where defenses soften, trust is re-established, and the most significant work happens, consolidating and integrating real change. Two days can be genuinely transformative, and more time only lets us go further.

Intensives are particularly effective for:

  • Couples in significant distress who need rapid stabilization
  • Couples who have tried weekly therapy before and felt it moved too slowly
  • Out-of-town clients or couples with limited scheduling flexibility
  • Individuals wanting to process a specific trauma or attachment wound in depth
  • Couples at a crossroads, not sure whether to continue the relationship or separate
  • Couples preparing for marriage who want to do substantial premarital work in a concentrated format
An intensive is not a shortcut, it is an immersion. The depth of work possible in a single focused day can equal or exceed what takes months in weekly sessions. That said, intensives are not suitable for everyone, all couples are assessed for fit before a booking is confirmed.

What you can imagine walking away with

Most couples arrive carrying months, sometimes years, of the same argument. Here is what many describe on the other side of two or three focused days together:

✓

Finally seeing the pattern underneath your fights, and how to step out of it together.

✓

Feeling like a team again, instead of two people managing a truce.

✓

Saying the things you have not been able to say, and being genuinely heard.

✓

Beginning to rebuild trust after betrayal or a long stretch of distance.

✓

A shared language for the hard moments, so the next conflict does not undo you.

✓

Reconnecting, emotionally and physically, and remembering why you chose each other.

This is the work I do best. When couples give the process real focus and show up fully, the change we reach together is often more than they imagined walking in.

Am I a good candidate for a therapy intensive?

Not every couple is ready for an intensive format, and I take that seriously. Before any intensive is confirmed, we have a complimentary consultation call to assess whether this format is appropriate for your situation. There are some circumstances where a slower, weekly pace is clinically safer and more appropriate, for example, when one or both partners are in acute crisis, when there is active domestic violence or safety concerns, or when the relational rupture is so significant that trust needs to be built more gradually first.

The assessment conversation is also where we clarify your goals, discuss what to expect, and determine which package and format is the right fit. This is not a barrier, it is how we make sure the intensive is genuinely useful rather than overwhelming.

A great fit if

  • ✓ You both want to work on the relationship, even if one of you is unsure it can be fixed
  • ✓ You are tired of slow progress and want to go deep, fast
  • ✓ Busy schedules make sustaining weekly sessions hard
  • ✓ You are facing a specific rupture, an affair, a betrayal, a long stretch of distance, and want focused repair
  • ✓ You value depth, discretion, and a highly trained therapist

Probably not the right time if

  • · One or both of you is in acute crisis and needs stabilization first
  • · There is active domestic violence, abuse, or a safety concern
  • · One partner has firmly decided to leave and is not open to the work (discernment counseling may be the better fit)
  • · You are not ready to be vulnerable or to sit with some discomfort

How does a therapy intensive work, step by step?

Here is what the intensive process looks like from inquiry to completion:

1

Complimentary suitability consultation

We begin with a 20-minute call to assess whether the intensive format is right for your situation, your goals, what you are navigating, your emotional readiness, and which format (two-day, three-day, or four-day) fits best. This is also where fees and logistics are confirmed.

2

Pre-session intake questionnaire

Before the intensive, each person completes a detailed intake questionnaire. This gives me a thorough picture of each partner's history, attachment patterns, and what they most hope to work through, so every hour together is used with intention rather than getting oriented in real time.

3

The intensive session itself

Sessions run in focused 90-minute blocks with short integration breaks between them. This pacing is deliberate, it allows the nervous system to settle between deep moments rather than rushing from one breakthrough to the next. For two-day intensives, Day 1 opens up the cycle and the underlying attachment dynamics; Day 2 goes deeper, consolidates the shifts, and builds new patterns of connection.

4

Written summary and next steps

After the intensive, you receive a written summary of the work covered, the key insights that emerged, and a clear set of next steps. This gives the work continuity, something concrete to return to in the weeks ahead when ordinary life resumes.

5

Follow-up sessions

Every intensive includes one or two follow-up 90-minute sessions to consolidate and extend the work. Many clients continue with regular sessions after the intensive, using it as a powerful launching point for ongoing growth rather than a standalone event.

Common questions

What is a couples therapy intensive?

A couples therapy intensive, sometimes called a marriage counseling intensive or weekend couples retreat, is a concentrated format of EFT couples therapy in two-day, three-day, or four-day sessions. It compresses months of weekly therapy into focused, uninterrupted work, designed for couples who need to move quickly, who have limited time for weekly sessions, or who are at a critical crossroads in their relationship.

Is a couples therapy intensive right for us?

Intensives work best when both partners are motivated and emotionally stable enough to sustain several hours of deep work. They are not recommended when there are active safety concerns, acute crisis, or when trust needs to be built more gradually. A complimentary suitability assessment is required before booking, I will give an honest recommendation about whether an intensive or weekly therapy is the better starting point.

How long does a therapy intensive last?

Intensives are available in three formats: two days, three days, and four days. I recommend at least two consecutive days, since the most significant work often happens on the second day once defenses have softened, and for most couples the three-day format is the one I recommend, the extra day consolidates, deepens, and integrates the change.

What formats are available for intensives in Brentwood TN?

I offer two-day, three-day, and four-day intensives via secure telehealth, plus two-day and three-day in-person intensives in the Brentwood and Nashville area, throughout Tennessee. Each is custom-designed around your specific situation and goals, and pricing is published on this page.

Telehealth intensives range from $2,675 (two-day) to $4,675 (four-day). In-person intensives in the Brentwood and Nashville area range from $3,675 (two-day) to $5,675 (four-day). Where do in-person intensives take place?

In a private, comfortable, confidential setting in downtown Nashville or Brentwood. Once your booking is confirmed, I share the exact location along with everything you need to prepare, timing, breaks, and what to bring, so you can arrive and simply focus on the work.
