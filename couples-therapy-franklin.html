Couples therapy and marriage counseling in Franklin, TN

From the outside, Franklin looks like a place where everything is working. It lands near the top of every “best place to live” list, its historic downtown draws people in just to walk it, and Williamson County shows up year after year among the wealthiest in the country. Underneath that, a lot of couples here are quietly worn out, and the very success that defines the place is part of the reason.

Most Franklin couples who reach out are not in open crisis. They are capable, accomplished people who have built an impressive life, careers, a home in a county where houses run deep into the $800,000s, children in one of the strongest school districts in the state, and somewhere in the middle of all that effort the two of them slowly turned into a well-run logistics company with a shared last name.

Franklin has also grown fast, and much of that growth is transplants who came for a corporate relocation or the schools and arrived without family or old friends nearby. When the marriage gets hard, there is often no one local who has known you long enough to lean on, and the polished surface of the community can make it feel like you are the only couple who is struggling.

Couples therapy exists for exactly this, not to rescue a marriage that is failing, but to rebuild the connection that made the whole effortful life feel worth it in the first place.

The particular strains of a Franklin marriage

A handful of pressures shape relationships in Williamson County in ways they do not in most places, and they tend to land quietly on a marriage.

The first is money, in a place where money is supposed to be a solved problem. Housing deep into the $800,000s, a cost of living around forty percent above the national average, childcare that can run well over a thousand dollars a month, all of it resting on two incomes that have to keep performing just to hold position. Financial strain is one of the most common things couples fight about anywhere; in Franklin it arrives wrapped in the expectation that people like you should have it handled.

The second is time, and the commute that eats it. A large share of Franklin’s workforce drives in and out every day, and for couples working in downtown Nashville the trip can stretch past an hour each way. By the time two people have absorbed the traffic, the inbox, and the evening activities calendar, there is very little of either of them left over for the other.

The third is the quiet pressure of keeping up. In one of the most affluent counties in the nation, there is a constant, mostly unspoken comparison, the houses, the trips, the kids’ travel teams, and a lifestyle that inflates to match. It is easy to pour everything into looking successful and lose the thread of actually being close to the person you are doing it all with.

And running underneath it, the schools. Williamson County’s schools are genuinely excellent and genuinely intense, and a great many couples organize their entire lives around their children’s achievement. It is a good thing taken to an exhausting degree, and it is striking how many couples realize, the first time the calendar clears for an hour, that they have not had a conversation that was not about the kids in months.

None of this means something is wrong with the two of you. It means you are living inside a specific set of pressures, and those pressures have quietly been shaping how you treat each other. That can change.

Why Franklin couples choose online couples therapy

When a week is already this full, the last thing that helps is adding another drive across town to sit in a waiting room. That one logistical hurdle is often what keeps couples from ever starting.

Online couples therapy takes it off the table. Sessions happen over a secure, HIPAA-compliant video platform, private and professional, from your own home once the kids are down, or from two separate screens when one of you is traveling or stuck late at the office in Cool Springs. The research here is settled: telehealth couples therapy produces outcomes equivalent to meeting in person.

For a community as visible and interconnected as Franklin, online also offers something quieter, discretion. No waiting room, no car in a lot someone recognizes. The work happens in your own space, on a schedule that bends around a real life rather than fighting it.

Choosing a couples therapist in Franklin: why the approach matters

There is no shortage of counselors in Williamson County, and “couples therapy,” “marriage counseling,” and “relationship therapy” can describe very different work depending on how the therapist trained. What predicts whether therapy actually helps is less the title on the sign than the method underneath it, and how thoroughly the therapist was trained in it.

I practice one model, and I practice it deeply: Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT), the couples approach with the strongest research base in the field. Instead of re-explaining it here, I have written it up plainly, what EFT is, where it parts ways with the Gottman Method, and a fuller overview of how I work with couples. The part that matters when you are choosing someone in Williamson County is this: EFT is not a set of communication drills laid over a struggling marriage. It works underneath, on whether the two of you can count on each other emotionally when it matters, which is the thing that actually changes daily life.

Roughly 75% of couples reach full recovery from distress through EFT, and 86 to 90% improve, the strongest outcome record of any couples approach (ICEEFT Research Summary). Just as important, the gains last, still deepening two years after the work ends.

Triple certification, uncommon in Williamson County

I am ICEEFT Triple-Certified in EFT for couples (EFCT), individuals (EFIT), and families (EFFT), a level held by relatively few therapists worldwide, and I am an EFT Supervisor Candidate who trains other clinicians in the model. I am not aware of another couples therapist currently serving the Franklin and Williamson County area who holds triple certification from ICEEFT.

Certification is not a marketing word. It takes hundreds of supervised hours and video review of real sessions by certified trainers. It is the difference between a therapist who has read about EFT and one who has been trained and evaluated in it.

Starting couples therapy from anywhere in Franklin

We begin with a free 20-minute video call, no pressure, just a first read on what is happening and whether I am the right person for it. If we go ahead, sessions are 50 minutes, weekly or every other week, from wherever your day allows, the kitchen island after bedtime, a home office between calls, or two separate screens when one of you is on the road.

The early work is mostly about seeing clearly. Together we slow the pattern the two of you keep repeating and name it out loud, so the pattern becomes the problem instead of your partner. That single reframe tends to take some heat out of things quickly. From there we go after what is driving the pattern underneath, the fear, the hurt, the wanting, and as those come into the open, the way you answer each other starts to change.

Most couples feel real movement inside 8 to 12 sessions. When the situation is urgent, a fresh betrayal, a rupture that will not wait, or a long backlog of distance you want addressed now, a couples intensive can fold months of weekly work into a few concentrated days.

How much does couples therapy cost in Franklin?

Pricing is simple and up front: one flat rate for everyone, in 60- and 90-minute options, with multi-day intensives available. I keep the current numbers, along with Good Faith Estimate details, on the fees section of my FAQ rather than letting figures drift out of date across the site. In a county where the cost of a divorce dwarfs the cost of therapy, couples work is one of the higher-return things two people can spend on.

I stay out of network on purpose. It keeps your records private and lets the work go deeper than insurance tends to allow. I can hand you a monthly superbill to submit for possible out-of-network reimbursement, and sessions are HSA and FSA eligible; I explain the full reasoning in why I work out of network.

Common questions

Do you offer couples therapy for Franklin couples?

Yes. Everything runs online, so I work with Franklin and Williamson County couples by secure video from anywhere in Tennessee. You will not drive to an office or sit in a waiting room; sessions take place on a private, HIPAA-compliant platform, on a schedule that fits around work and the kids.

How does online couples therapy work for busy Franklin professionals?

You and your partner log in to a private video room, side by side on one screen or from two devices. Sessions last 50 minutes and usually run weekly or every other week, easy to slot between Cool Springs meetings or after the evening activities are done. The format does not water anything down; studies consistently put telehealth couples work on par with sitting in an office.

Can my partner and I join from two different locations?

Yes, and it is common here. When one of you is commuting, away on a work trip, or simply cannot get to the same room by session time, each of you can join from wherever you are. Being in separate places does not stop the work, and it is one of the reasons online suits two-career Williamson County households so well.

Which Williamson County communities do you serve?

All of them, because the work is online. I see couples across Williamson County, Franklin, Cool Springs, Brentwood, Nolensville, Thompson's Station, Spring Hill, and Fairview, and anywhere else in Tennessee. If you live in the county, or anywhere in the state, you attend from home.

We look like we have it all together. Is couples therapy still worth it?

Especially then. In a community as high-achieving as Franklin, plenty of couples are experts at looking fine and quietly exhausted underneath it. Therapy is not an admission of failure; it is what people who take their marriage as seriously as their careers actually do. What you say in the room stays private, and the work is often most powerful for couples who have held things together for a long time.

How much does couples therapy cost in Franklin?

Sessions run at one flat rate for individuals and couples, in 60- and 90-minute options, with multi-day intensives when a couple needs to move faster. The current numbers live on the fees section of my FAQ. I stay out of network, provide a monthly superbill you can submit for possible out-of-network reimbursement, and accept HSA and FSA funds.
