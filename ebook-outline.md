## Positioning

A quick decision worth making up front: Marni's guide speaks to "marriage" broadly. Your site speaks to high-achieving couples who are quietly losing each other. My recommendation is to keep the guide broadly useful (it answers universal questions, which widens the top of your funnel) while letting your voice, examples, and asides lean toward the high-achiever reality. That way the guide self-selects your ideal reader without shutting anyone out. If you'd rather aim it squarely at high-achieving couples, I can tighten every chapter to that lens.

## The arc (why this order)

A cold reader isn't ready to book. They're hurting, unsure, and full of objections. So the guide moves the way a real decision moves: *recognize yourself → drop the objections → understand what's actually wrong → see it clearly through a familiar lens → learn how to choose well → understand the cost → learn how to make it work → take one small step.* The consult ask comes only at the end, after you've earned it by being useful.

## Chapter 1 — Is it actually time to get help?

*Purpose:* help the reader recognize themselves on the page.
- The quiet signs: living like roommates, feeling like opponents, the same fight on a loop, one of you stopped trying.
- The six-year wait (couples wait an average of six years before reaching out) and why sooner is easier.
- An honest boundary: when couples therapy is *not* the right first step (active abuse, coercive control, danger). Safety first.
- *Source:* Marni's myths intro + your safety framing. *Search intent:* "signs we need marriage counseling."

## Chapter 2 — The myths that keep good couples stuck

*Purpose:* dismantle the objections that stop people from booking.
- Adapt Marni's seven myths, warmed into your voice: therapy has a poor success rate; it takes forever; I'll be blamed; my partner needs individual therapy first; the therapist will decide if we split; it's too late for us; talking will make it worse.
- Fold in the EFT evidence honestly: APA-recognized, roughly 75% of couples reaching full recovery, replicated over 40 years. This is where your certification quietly earns credibility.
- *Source:* Marni ch. 2 + your EFT material. *Search intent:* "does couples therapy work," "will I be blamed in couples therapy."

## Chapter 3 — What's actually happening between you: the cycle

*Purpose:* the "oh, that's us" chapter. This is your differentiation, and it's not in Marni's book.
- The negative cycle: pursuer and withdrawer, and how each move makes the other worse.
- Attachment underneath it: the softer emotions (fear, hurt, loneliness, longing) driving the surface fight.
- A brief, generous nod to your original framework, Asymmetrical Repair Syndrome, for couples where one partner has been carrying the repair alone. Positions you as an original thinker, not a repackager.
- Link readers to your EFT glossary and the pursuer-withdrawer article.
- *Source:* your own work (pursuer-withdrawer, ARS, glossary). *Search intent:* "why do we keep having the same fight," "pursuer withdrawer pattern." *This is your strongest AEO chapter, the part AI answer engines will quote.*

## Chapter 4 — The Four Horsemen, and what's underneath them

*Purpose:* meet readers at the framework they may already know, then deepen it with your lens.
- Adapt Marni's Gottman chapter: criticism, contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling, with the antidotes.
- Your bridge: each "horseman" is a move in the cycle, and under each one is a softer emotion trying to be heard. Gottman names the *what*; EFT gets to the *why*.
- *Source:* Marni ch. 5 (Gottman). *Attribution to the Gottmans required regardless of Marni's permission.* *Search intent:* "four horsemen of divorce."

## Chapter 5 — How to choose a couples therapist who can actually help

*Purpose:* useful, practical, and it lets your credentials land naturally.
- What the licenses mean (LMFT, LCSW, LPC, psychologist) and why specialized couples training matters.
- The questions to ask before you book, and reputable directories (ICEEFT, AAMFT, Gottman, Psychology Today).
- Trust your gut in the first few sessions; it's okay to change therapists.
- *Source:* Marni ch. 1. *Search intent:* "how to find a good marriage counselor," "questions to ask a couples therapist."

## Chapter 6 — What couples therapy costs, and why

*Purpose:* handle the money objection honestly, in your Tennessee, private-pay context.
- Why specialists cost more (years of training, supervision, certification).
- The insurance reality: relationship work usually has no billable diagnosis; the ethics of not pathologizing one partner; how superbills work.
- Why sessions often run 75–90 minutes, and why "cheaper than divorce" is more than a slogan.
- Honest lower-cost alternatives (workshops, training clinics, self-help), stated plainly.
- *Source:* Marni ch. 4 + your fee/FAQ context. *Search intent:* "why is couples therapy so expensive," "does insurance cover marriage counseling."

## Chapter 7 — How to make therapy actually work

*Purpose:* set expectations and hand the reader agency.
- Adapt Marni's ten tips, warmed: same goal, real commitment, no "D word" as a weapon, work on yourself, drop "I'm doing all the work," don't plan a silent exit, choose confidants carefully, prioritize it, understand the cycle, bring your best self.
- A soft forward-tease: "When we begin working together, I'll share a fuller readiness guide, the commitments that make repair possible." (This is your intake 21-commitments piece, kept as a client-only asset, which makes booking feel like the next real step.)
- *Source:* Marni ch. 3 + your commitments work. *Search intent:* "how to get the most out of couples therapy."
